I know the law, sir.

ROBERT
Then you know I can't help you.

(beat)

But I'll tell you one thing for free. Whoever did this to him wasn't one of his people. His people loved him.

Marc, across the table, puts down his fork. Just for a moment, he looks at his father.

Elxa catches it. She doesn't know what she caught.

NATASHA
(rising, brisk)
Who wants peach cobbler?

MARC-ANTHONY
(too quickly)
Not peach.

Everyone looks at him.

MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
(recovering, grinning)
I'm on a diet. I'm thirty-four now. My knees make noises.

NATASHA
(fond)
You're ridiculous.

MARC-ANTHONY
I'm the President of the company, Mama. I'm ridiculous professionally now.

The table laughs. Elxa laughs too. But she keeps watching her brother.

In the kitchen a minute later, Natasha spoons cobbler into bowls. Elxa leans against the counter with her arms crossed.

NATASHA
Your husband looks at you like a question.

ELXA
What does that mean?

NATASHA
It means he's been watching you all night like he's trying to solve you.

ELXA
He's a detective. He watches everybody like that.

NATASHA
Not like that.

(beat)

Did you two talk about babies yet?

ELXA
Mama.

NATASHA
I'm fifty-four. I'm allowed to ask one question a year.

ELXA
Every time I bring it up, he says "after the case."

NATASHA
There's always a case.

ELXA
(quietly)
That's what I said.

Natasha stops spooning. She looks at her eldest, the Chief of Police, standing in her mother's kitchen looking twelve years old.

NATASHA
Your father said "after the bar exam." Then "after I make partner." Then "after I open my own firm."

ELXA
And?

NATASHA
And then you came anyway. Before any of it. You didn't wait for anybody's "after."

(handing her a bowl)

Sometimes the best things in this family arrive early, baby. Ask your grandfather. He hated it. Look how you turned out.

Elxa laughs. It's small, but it's real. She takes the bowl.

CUT TO:

EXT. BULLOCK ESTATE - BACK PORCH - NIGHT (8:40 PM)

A wide stone porch overlooking the dark lawn. Robert sits in a rocking chair with a cigar he isn't supposed to have. His breath and the smoke mingle in the cold.

The screen door creaks. Marc steps out with two cups of coffee, hands one to his father, and sits in the other rocker.

ROBERT
Your mother send you out here to make me put this out?

MARC-ANTHONY
She sent me out here to see if you'd put it out.

ROBERT
And?

MARC-ANTHONY
And I'm going to tell her you did.

ROBERT
(pleased)
That's my boy.

They rock. The cold settles over them. Somewhere down the ridge a dog barks.

ROBERT (CONT'D)
You didn't want the cobbler.

MARC-ANTHONY
Diet.

ROBERT
You ate three helpings of short ribs.

MARC-ANTHONY
Protein diet.

Robert takes a slow pull on the cigar.

ROBERT
Terrence's mother used to bring peach cobbler to the courthouse. Every day of his trial. Cut it into squares on paper plates for the bailiffs, the clerks, the court reporter. Tried to give a piece to the prosecutor once.

MARC-ANTHONY
(quiet)
Did he take it?

ROBERT
He did. Then he lost.

(beat)

Best cobbler I ever had in my life.

Marc doesn't answer. He looks out at the dark.

ROBERT (CONT'D)
(still looking at the lawn, casual)
Funny thing, you turning down cobbler tonight.

MARC-ANTHONY
(lightly)
Is it?

ROBERT
I've been cross-examining people for thirty years, son. I know what it looks like when somebody decides not to say something.

MARC-ANTHONY
(beat)
And what does it look like?

ROBERT
(turning to look at him)
It looks exactly like you right now.

Marc meets his father's eyes. Then he smiles, the big warm famous smile.

MARC-ANTHONY
Daddy. I'm an actor. I decide not to say things for a living.

Robert studies him a long moment. Then he laughs, low and genuine, and taps the ash off his cigar.

ROBERT
That's what I'm afraid of.

The screen door bangs open.

NATASHA (O.S.)
Robert Bullock, I can smell that from the kitchen!

ROBERT
(stubbing it out instantly)
It's Marc's!

MARC-ANTHONY
(standing, delighted)
And there it is. Thirty years of legal training.

CUT TO:

INT. SMILLEY ESTATE - TWINS' WALK-IN CLOSET - NIGHT (9:15 PM)

A walk-in closet the size of a studio apartment, lined with matching uniforms, sneakers, and a vanity. The door is shut. A single ring light glows on the floor.

Emma and Victoria sit cross-legged in pajamas. Between them, a stack of Esther's old permission slips, field-trip forms, and a birthday card, all signed.

Victoria has a pad of paper. On it, twenty attempts at the same signature: Esther C. Smilley.

VICTORIA
(holding up the pad)
Okay. Which one?

EMMA
(looking, reluctantly)
Number fourteen.

VICTORIA
Fourteen's good, right?

EMMA
Fourteen is scary good. That's the problem.

VICTORIA
The "E" is the whole thing. She does this loop, like she's lassoing something.

EMMA
Tori. We said just to see.

VICTORIA
I'm just seeing. I'm seeing if my hand can do it. That's not a crime. That's calligraphy.

Emma picks up the birthday card. Their mother's handwriting: "To my two whole hearts. Love you past the moon, Mom."

EMMA
(soft)
She's going to be so mad.

VICTORIA
Only if she finds out.

EMMA
She always finds out.

VICTORIA
(setting down the pen, serious for once)
Em. Do you ever feel like there's a room in this house we're not allowed in?

EMMA
There are, like, forty rooms we're not allowed in. Grandpa Gus's study. Grandpa Leonard's wine cellar...

VICTORIA
Not a real room. A room in Mom.

Emma doesn't answer. Outside the closet door, footsteps pass in the hallway. Both girls freeze. The footsteps keep going. A door closes.

VICTORIA (CONT'D)
(whisper)
Every time somebody says his name, she goes into that room and shuts the door.

EMMA
(whisper)
Maybe she just really hates him.

VICTORIA
Maybe.

(beat)

Or maybe she really doesn't.

The girls look at each other in the ring light. Emma reaches over, takes the pen, and, very slowly and carefully, writes the signature herself.

Esther C. Smilley.

It's perfect.

EMMA
(staring at it)
Oh no.

VICTORIA
(delighted)
You're better than me!

A KNOCK on the closet door. Both girls jump.

ESTHER (O.S.)
Girls? Lights out was twenty minutes ago.

Victoria shoves the pad under a pile of sweaters. Emma sits on the permission slips.

VICTORIA
(sweetly)
We're picking out outfits for tomorrow!

ESTHER (O.S.)
You wear uniforms.

VICTORIA
We're picking out which uniform!

A pause on the other side of the door. The girls hold their breath.

ESTHER (O.S.)
(softer)
I love you. Both of you. You know that?

The twins look at each other. Something in their mother's voice.

EMMA
(quiet)
We know, Mom.

ESTHER (O.S.)
Bed.

Her footsteps go. The girls exhale. Emma looks down at the page in her lap with the perfect signature on it.

EMMA
(tearing the page out, balling it up)
Nobody saw that.

VICTORIA
(grinning)
I definitely saw that.

CUT TO:

INT. APD HEADQUARTERS - HOMICIDE SQUAD ROOM - NIGHT (9:38 PM)

The room is empty except for Jeremy and Rosa. Desk lamps. Takeout containers. The whiteboard: T-BONE, ARCHITECT = ?, and now a list of fourteen dates and times.

On Jeremy's desk, in a clear evidence bag, the black flip phone. It's plugged into a charging cable that runs out of a slit in the bag. The screen glows faintly.

Jeremy types a warrant affidavit with two fingers. Rosa paces.

ROSA
Two days.

JEREMY
Maybe tomorrow if Okoye's in a good mood.

ROSA
Okoye has never been in a good mood. Okoye was born in a bad mood and has maintained it professionally for sixty years.

JEREMY
Then two days.

Rosa stops pacing. She looks at the phone glowing in its bag.

ROSA
It's charged.

JEREMY
(not looking up)
No.

ROSA
I didn't say anything.

JEREMY
You were thinking about it very loudly.

ROSA
It's one call. The number's in the phone. The phone's in evidence. He's not going to complain.

JEREMY
We don't know who's on the other end. If we call it, we tell them we have the phone.

ROSA
Or we find out if it's even on.

JEREMY
We wait for the warrant.

ROSA
And in two days, whoever strangled T-Bone Gaines with his bare hands buys a new phone and a plane ticket.

Jeremy stops typing. He looks at the phone. He looks at his partner.

JEREMY
Delgado.

ROSA
Jackson.

JEREMY
If you touch that phone, I have to write it up.

ROSA
Then write it up.

She pulls on a latex glove. Reaches into the evidence bag. Opens the flip phone. Scrolls to the only contact.

ARCHITECT.

She presses CALL. Puts it on speaker.

A pause. A click.

Then, somewhere on a cell network, it begins to RING.

Jeremy stands up so fast his chair rolls into the wall.

CUT TO:

INT. MARC-ANTHONY'S ESTATE - LYRIC'S ROOM - NIGHT (9:00 PM)

Lyric in bed, face washed, bonnet on, a script on her lap she isn't reading. Marc sits in the armchair beside the bed, still in his dinner clothes, napkin finally out of his collar.

LYRIC
Uncle Marc. Can I ask you something hypothetical?

MARC-ANTHONY
My favorite kind of question.

LYRIC
Hypothetically. If a person really, really wanted something. Like, knew in their whole body it was the thing they were supposed to do. But somebody who loved them said no.

MARC-ANTHONY
(careful)
Hypothetically.

LYRIC
Totally hypothetically.

MARC-ANTHONY
Is this person a thirteen-year-old in a Westbrook blazer who wears her blazer inside out?

LYRIC
(too fast)
I don't know anybody like that.

MARC-ANTHONY
(smiling)
Okay.

(beat)

Hypothetically. I'd tell that person that wanting something isn't a crime. It's the best thing about being alive.

LYRIC
But?

MARC-ANTHONY
But the person who said no loves them too. And they might have a reason the person can't see yet.

LYRIC
What if the reason's dumb?

MARC-ANTHONY
Sometimes it is. Grown-ups have a lot of dumb reasons. I've got a few.

LYRIC
Like what?

MARC-ANTHONY
I won't eat peach cobbler anymore.

LYRIC
Why?

MARC-ANTHONY
(beat)
Because it reminds me of somebody.

Lyric looks at him. She's smart enough to know she's just been told something real, and young enough not to know what it was.

LYRIC
Is that a sad reason?

MARC-ANTHONY
(standing, kissing her forehead)
It's a grown-up reason. Go to sleep, Chef.

LYRIC
(sleepy, sliding down under the covers)
You're going to let them audition, right? Hypothetically.

MARC-ANTHONY
(at the door)
Hypothetically, everybody gets a shot.

(beat)

Actually, not hypothetically. Actually.

He turns off the light.

CUT TO:

INT. MARC-ANTHONY'S ESTATE - STUDY - NIGHT (9:41 PM)

Dark, except for one desk lamp. Marc, back from dinner, sits reading the Crowns script. The house is quiet. Lyric is asleep upstairs.

A sound.

A low, insistent BUZZ from inside the desk. From the locked top drawer.

Marc goes very still.

He sets down the fountain pen. He takes a small key from his pocket and unlocks the drawer.

Inside: a plain black phone. No case. No brand. A phone that almost never rings, because almost nobody in the world has the number.

It's ringing now. The screen glows.

The caller ID reads: T-BONE.

Marc stares at it. A dead man is calling him.

The phone buzzes in his hand. Once. Twice. Three times.

Marc doesn't answer. He doesn't move. He watches the name on the screen the way a man watches a snake.

The buzzing stops. The screen goes dark.

CUT TO:

INT. APD HEADQUARTERS - HOMICIDE SQUAD ROOM - CONTINUOUS (9:41 PM)

The call rings out. The flip phone's screen: CALL ENDED. 0:00.

Rosa lowers it slowly. Jeremy stands across from her, furious and silent.

ROSA
It's on.

JEREMY
(quiet, controlled)
You just told whoever that is that we have his phone.

ROSA
I just found out it's alive. That's more than we knew an hour ago.

JEREMY
You made them nervous.

ROSA
Good. Nervous people make mistakes.

JEREMY
(beat)
Nervous people run.

He takes the phone out of her gloved hand, drops it back into the evidence bag, and seals it.

JEREMY (CONT'D)
And now I have to write it up.

He sits back down at his desk and starts typing, harder than before. Rosa looks at the whiteboard. ARCHITECT = ?

ROSA
(softly)
Whoever you are. You just saw his name come up on your phone.

(beat)

I hope it scared you.

CUT TO:

INT. MARC-ANTHONY'S ESTATE - STUDY - CONTINUOUS (9:43 PM)

Marc still holds the black phone. He hasn't moved.

He turns it over. He takes out the battery with steady fingers. Sets the battery on one side of the desk and the phone on the other.

Then he picks up his regular phone and dials. It rings once.

KANE (V.O.)
(filtered)
Yeah.

MARC-ANTHONY
(very calm)
Kane. Somebody just called me.

KANE (V.O.)
Who?

MARC-ANTHONY
(beat)
T-Bone.

Silence on the line.

MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
From a phone I never gave him. On a number he should never have had.

(beat)

Wake up Zeke. Wake up everybody.

He hangs up. He sits alone in the lamplight, looking at the two halves of the black phone lying on his desk.

His face is perfectly calm. His eyes are not.

MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
(whisper)
Who gave you my number, T?

HOLD on Marc in the dark.

FADE OUT.
