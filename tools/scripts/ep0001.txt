So anyway. I made him pancakes this morning and they were really, really bad. And he ate one anyway. Because he says a man eats what his family cooks him.

(beat)

So. Happy birthday to the best man I know. You're my family.

She sets the microphone down. The room explodes into applause. Marc crosses the dance floor, lifts her off the stage in a hug, and spins her around. People are crying. Joan is crying. Even Victor, at the edge of the room, dabs at one eye with a pocket square.

At the back of the room, Harmony presses her fingers hard against her mouth. Amond, behind her, doesn't say a word. He just puts a hand on her shoulder.

She doesn't shake it off.

Near the bar, Martin applauds too, slowly, in black leather gloves.

Peter, beside him, looks down at those gloves. Then up at his son's face.

PETER
(quiet)
You should take those off.

MARTIN
(still smiling, still clapping)
You should mind your business.

CUT TO:

INT. SUMMIT HOUSE - SERVICE CORRIDOR - NIGHT (9:30 PM)

A narrow back passage behind the kitchen. Waiters rush past with trays. Marc stands against the wall, still wiping his eyes with the heel of his hand, when Kane appears beside him.

MARC-ANTHONY
Don't say anything about the crying.

KANE
I wasn't going to.

MARC-ANTHONY
I wasn't crying. I had a pancake in my eye. It's been in there since seven a.m.

KANE
(quiet)
The man in the river. APD's got his time of death around eleven-forty last night. Strangled. Bare hands. Somebody knelt on him.

Marc's face goes still.

MARC-ANTHONY
Any name?

KANE
Not yet. Amond still can't find T-Bone.

A waiter squeezes past. They wait until he's gone.

MARC-ANTHONY
Tell Zeke. Tonight. I want to know everyone who walked across that bridge last night. Everyone who drove over it. Every camera between here and the river.

KANE
And if it's him?

MARC-ANTHONY
(beat)
Then somebody took one of ours on my birthday.

He straightens his bow tie. He checks his reflection in the dark window of a service door. The tears are gone. His eyes are clear and very, very calm.

MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
And I'm going to want to meet them.

He pushes back through the door into the light and noise of the party. By the time he's on the other side he's laughing again.

Kane watches him go. Then he takes out his phone and makes a call.

KANE
(into phone)
It's me. Wake up Zeke.

== ACT FIVE

INT. SUMMIT HOUSE - GRAND FOYER - NIGHT (10:00 PM)

Just before ten. A quiet alcove off the foyer behind a curtain of white roses. Natasha adjusts Marc's pocket square with the precision of a surgeon.

NATASHA
Stand up straight.

MARC-ANTHONY
I am standing up straight.

NATASHA
Stand up straighter. You're about to be on every television in the world.

MARC-ANTHONY
Mama, I'm on every television in the world every day. I'm in a yogurt commercial in Japan.

NATASHA
Not like this.

She stops fussing. Looks at him. Her baby boy, thirty-four years old, the most famous man alive.

NATASHA (CONT'D)
When he's done, they'll call you "sir" at the Tower. People twice your age will stand up when you walk in. And half of them will be praying you fail.

MARC-ANTHONY
Only half?

NATASHA
The other half will be helping.

MARC-ANTHONY
(smiling)
You're so encouraging. You should do motivational speaking.

NATASHA
(serious)
Marc-Anthony. I bend rules for a living. You know that. I taught you that.

MARC-ANTHONY
You taught me everything.

NATASHA
Then learn this one too. Never let them see you bend. Let them think you're the sweetest man in the room.

(beat)

Because you are. And that's the best disguise there is.

He kisses her forehead. She pats his cheek, twice, hard, like she did when he was little and about to go onstage.

NATASHA (CONT'D)
Go get your crown, baby.

The whole party has gathered below the grand staircase. DBC cameras on risers. Five hundred faces turned up toward the landing.

Victor stands at the top with Joan beside him. Below them, on the first landing, the family has been arranged the way families are arranged for photographs: Natasha and Robert, Jasmine and Peter, Elxa and Jeremy, Grace, Mallory, Martin.

And in the center, Marc-Anthony, with Lyric's hand in his.

VICTOR
Eighty-seven years ago, my father set four pages of type in a back room on Auburn Avenue. He had a few hundred dollars, a secondhand press, and a woman who believed in him enough to pay for it.

(beat)

That woman was my mother, Simone. And she's the reason every one of us is standing here tonight.

Applause. Joan squeezes his hand.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
They told my father a Black man couldn't own a newspaper in Georgia. He owned four. They told him a Black family couldn't own a ridge above the river. You're standing on it.

Applause, louder.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
My father built a newspaper. I built a company. My daughter Natasha built it into the most valuable enterprise the world has ever seen.

(turning to Natasha)

Thank you, baby.

Natasha raises her glass. Jasmine's smile does not move a millimeter.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
But a company is only a building until somebody decides who gets to hold the keys. And tonight, on my grandson's thirty-fourth birthday, I've decided.

The foyer goes utterly silent.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
Effective immediately, the board of The Donohue Company has appointed Marc-Anthony Bullock as its President and Chief Operating Officer.

A gasp ripples through the room. Then applause starts, uncertain at first, and builds into a roar. Cameras flash. Grace shrieks. Lyric jumps up and down.

Victor raises his hand. Silence again.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
And this morning, I signed over to him eight hundred million Class B shares of this company, with every vote that comes with them.

(beat)

He is the first of my grandchildren to hold a vote in this family's future.

A different sound now. Not a gasp. An intake of breath from the people who understand what that means.

On the landing, Martin's hand closes around his champagne flute.

VICTOR (CONT'D)
This family has a covenant. We take care of what we've built. We give back more than we take. And we pass the crown to the one who will carry it the farthest.

(looking at Marc)

Son. It's yours to carry.

Marc looks up at his grandfather. For once, he has nothing funny to say.

MARC-ANTHONY
(barely audible)
Yes, sir.

The room explodes. Applause, cheers, the quartet striking up a fanfare.

And on the landing, in the middle of the applause, Martin's champagne flute SHATTERS in his gloved hand.

Glass rains onto the marble. Champagne runs down his wrist. And through the black leather, slowly, a dark stain spreads.

Blood.

Guests nearby turn. Mallory gasps. Jasmine grabs his arm.

JASMINE
Martin!

MARTIN
(calm, smiling)
It's nothing. Cheap glass.

Peter is already moving, pushing through the family toward his son. Martin holds up his bleeding hand to the room, still smiling.

MARTIN (CONT'D)
(loud, bright)
To my cousin!

The crowd, relieved, laughs and raises their glasses.

CROWD
To Marc-Anthony!

Across the landing, Marc and Martin lock eyes. Nobody else sees it. Marc isn't smiling anymore. Neither is Martin.

CUT TO:

INT. SUMMIT HOUSE - POWDER ROOM - NIGHT (10:08 PM)

Marble sink, gold fixtures. Martin sits on the vanity stool with his glove off. Peter kneels in front of him with a first-aid kit, cleaning the wound.

The gauze beneath is soaked through. Three of the eight black stitches have torn open.

PETER
These stitches were twenty hours old.

MARTIN
Then you did a bad job.

PETER
(tying off)
I did a perfect job. At two in the morning. In the middle of Sunday night. On my own son, who wouldn't tell me what he hit.

MARTIN
A wall.

PETER
Walls don't bruise like that.

MARTIN
This one did.

Peter looks up at him. Martin meets his gaze with perfect, polite calm.

PETER
Martin. If you're in trouble...

MARTIN
I'm not in trouble, Dad.

(standing, flexing the fresh bandage)

I'm in line.

He pulls the ruined glove back on over the bandage and walks out. Peter kneels alone on the marble floor, holding a wad of bloody gauze.

He looks at it for a long moment.

Then he drops it into the trash and washes his hands for a long, long time.

CUT TO:

INT. SMILLEY ESTATE - ESTHER'S BEDROOM - NIGHT (10:10 PM)

Esther sits in the dark again, laptop open, the DBC live feed on screen. We hear Victor's voice, tinny and far away: "...President and Chief Operating Officer..."

On screen, Marc-Anthony stands on the staircase, stunned and golden.

Esther watches without moving.

On her nightstand, turned toward the bed, is a framed photo: two little girls, around six years old, in matching Easter dresses, grinning with the same crooked grin.

Esther looks from the screen to the photo. From the photo to the screen.

Down the hall, the music has stopped. A door creaks. Small footsteps. Esther quickly angles the laptop away from the door.

EMMA (O.S.)
(sleepy, through the door)
Mom? Are you watching the Donohue thing?

ESTHER
(too quickly)
No, baby. Go to sleep.

VICTORIA (O.S.)
She's totally watching it.

EMMA (O.S.)
Shh!

Giggles. Footsteps retreating. A door closes.

Esther lets out a breath she's been holding for fourteen years.

She closes her eyes.

ESTHER
(barely a whisper)
Not yet.

She shuts the laptop.

CUT TO:

INT. SUMMIT HOUSE - VICTOR'S STUDY - NIGHT (10:30 PM)

The party roars on beyond the closed doors. Inside, quiet. Victor pours three fingers of fifty-year-old scotch into three glasses. Joan sits in a wingback chair. Marc stands in the middle of the room like a man who's just been hit by a very expensive truck.

VICTOR
Sit down before you fall down.

MARC-ANTHONY
I'm fine.

JOAN
You're swaying.

MARC-ANTHONY
It's the scotch.

VICTOR
You haven't had any.

MARC-ANTHONY
I'm anticipating it.

He sits. Victor hands him a glass.

MARC-ANTHONY (CONT'D)
Granddaddy. I didn't ask for this.

VICTOR
That's why you got it. Anybody who asks for a crown is exactly who shouldn't have one.

MARC-ANTHONY
Martin asked for it.

VICTOR
Martin's been asking since he was thirteen.

(beat)

Martin pushed you down my staircase on Christmas night. You think I didn't see it?

Marc goes very still.

MARC-ANTHONY
You told everybody it was an accident.

VICTOR
I told everybody what the family needed to hear. You broke your arm. He broke something worse in himself. I've been watching him ever since.

JOAN
Victor.

VICTOR
He needs to know, Joan. He's not a boy anymore.

(to Marc)

You have a good heart. The best in this family. That's why you'll carry it farther than any of us.

(beat)

But a good heart is also the first thing they'll try to cut out of you.

Marc looks down into his scotch. When he looks up, there's something in his eyes that neither of his grandparents has quite seen before. Calm. Clear. Old.

MARC-ANTHONY
They can try.

Victor studies him a long moment. Then he raises his glass.

VICTOR
To the future.

JOAN
To the family.

MARC-ANTHONY
(grinning, the boy again)
To the peacocks.

VICTOR
What peacocks?

JOAN
(sighing)
Your son sent peacocks.

VICTOR
(closing his eyes)
Of course he did.

A knock. The door opens. Natasha and Robert slip in, shoes in hand, both of them a little breathless from the dance floor.

NATASHA
There you are. I've been looking for my son. The President.

ROBERT
Chief Operating Officer. Say the whole title, Tasha. We paid for it.

VICTOR
You didn't pay for anything.

ROBERT
Emotionally, Victor. Emotionally I paid for it.

Marc laughs and stands. Robert pulls him into a hug and holds on a beat longer than usual.

ROBERT (CONT'D)
(low, just for Marc)
Proud of you. You know that?

MARC-ANTHONY
I know, Daddy.

ROBERT
(pulling back, back to business)
Now. As your attorney. Never sign anything your grandfather hands you without me reading it first.

VICTOR
Robert.

ROBERT
As his father, I stand by it.

Joan laughs, finally, and holds out her glass. Victor pours scotch for everyone.

They drink.

== ACT SIX

EXT. SUMMIT HOUSE - MOTOR COURT - NIGHT (11:10 PM)

The party is emptying out. Valets run. Guests stumble to cars, laughing. Somewhere in the east garden a peacock SCREAMS. Several guests scream back.

Elxa and Jeremy wait at the curb for the Tahoe. Jeremy's phone buzzes. He answers instantly.

JEREMY
(into phone)
Jackson... Say that again.

He listens. His face changes.

JEREMY (CONT'D)
Don't touch it. I'm on my way.

He hangs up.

ELXA
What?

JEREMY
The M.E. went back through his clothes. Sewn into the lining of that coat. A second phone. A burner.

ELXA
(beat)
Somebody sewed a phone into his coat.

JEREMY
Somebody who didn't want it found.

ELXA
Or somebody who did.

The Tahoe pulls up. Jeremy opens her door, then hesitates.

JEREMY
I'll take a squad car. You go home.

ELXA
I'm the Chief of Police, Jeremy. I don't go home.

JEREMY
(gently)
You do tonight. You're in a ball gown and you've had four glasses of your grandfather's champagne.

ELXA
Three.

JEREMY
Three and a half. I counted.

She wants to argue. She doesn't.

ELXA
Call me the second you know who he is.

JEREMY
You'll be the first.

He kisses her quickly and jogs off toward a waiting patrol car. Elxa stands alone in the cold under the blazing house.

At the top of the steps, Grace catches Marc by the sleeve before he can leave.

GRACE
Hey.

MARC-ANTHONY
Hey.

GRACE
I saw how you and Martin looked at each other up there.

MARC-ANTHONY
He cut his hand. I was concerned.

GRACE
(flat)
Uh-huh.

(beat)

Whatever that was. Whatever it turns into. You call me first.

MARC-ANTHONY
Gracie. What are you going to do, sing at him?

GRACE
I own a shovel, Marc-Anthony. I own a very nice shovel. And I don't ask questions.

He looks at his baby sister in her emerald gown and diamonds, dead serious, and he pulls her into a hug.

MARC-ANTHONY
(into her hair)
I know you don't.

She lets go. He goes back for Lyric.

A Maybach pulls up for the Olsons. Peter opens the door for Jasmine. She pauses, one foot in the car, and looks back up at the blazing house.

JASMINE
Tomorrow morning, I'm going to see my father.

PETER
Jasmine, let it breathe for one night.

JASMINE
I let it breathe for fifty-five years, Peter.

(beat)

Tomorrow it's going to hear me.

She gets in. Martin slides in beside her without a word, cradling his bandaged hand. The door shuts. The car glides away into the dark.

Behind her, at the top of the steps, Marc walks Lyric out to a waiting car, a sleepy thirteen-year-old slumped against his side. Kane walks one step behind.

Elxa turns and watches the three of them go down the steps into the dark.

She doesn't know why she can't look away.

CUT TO:

INT. HARMONY'S APARTMENT - ADAMSVILLE - NIGHT (11:40 PM)

A small one-bedroom in southwest Atlanta. Film posters for movies that were never made. A secondhand desk. A space heater ticking in the corner.

Harmony, still in the black dress, kicks off her heels and sits down at the desk. She opens the worn laptop from her camera bag.

She logs in. The screen shows a sleek pink-and-black blog dashboard.

THE TEA KETTLE ATL.

Two point one million followers.

Harmony cracks her knuckles. And she begins to type. We see the words appear on screen as she writes them.

INSERT - LAPTOP SCREEN

The cursor blinks. Then the words appear:

"BLIND ITEM: Which ridge royal broke a champagne glass with his bare hand tonight when Granddaddy handed the crown to his cousin? Word is the blood matched his gloves. And an UPDATE on this morning's item: our Tuxedo Park princess stayed home AGAIN. Fourteen years of birthday invitations, fourteen no-shows. Some allergies run deep... #KettleIsOn #RidgeVsThePark"

BACK TO SCENE

Harmony reads it over. She hesitates over the second item. Her finger hovers over the delete key.

She thinks of Esther on the terrace: "It's genetic."

She leaves it in.

She clicks PUBLISH.

The post goes live. Within seconds the counters begin to spin. Likes. Shares. Comments. Hundreds. Thousands.

Her phone buzzes on the desk. A text. The name on the screen: ESTHER.

INSERT - HARMONY'S PHONE

ESTHER: "Did you see the Kettle??? 'Fourteen years.' Who COUNTS that?"

BACK TO SCENE

Harmony stares at the text. She glances at the laptop. Back at the phone.

She types with one thumb.

INSERT - HARMONY'S PHONE

HARMONY: "idk. weirdos"

BACK TO SCENE

She hits send, sets the phone face-down, and presses both hands over her face.

Harmony leans back in her chair in the ticking dark, watching the numbers climb, her face lit blue by the screen.

HARMONY
(softly, to herself)
Nobody takes me seriously.

(beat)

Everybody reads me.

CUT TO:

INT. MARC-ANTHONY'S ESTATE - LYRIC'S ROOM - NIGHT (11:52 PM)

Dark. Lyric is asleep in her gala dress, on top of the covers, one shoe still on.

Marc eases the shoe off. Pulls a blanket over her. He picks up the small wrapped box from her nightstand, the gift from this morning, and sits on the floor beside the bed, back against the wall, the way he did every night when she was eight.

He unwraps it. Inside is a keychain: a small silver disk, engraved.

He turns it toward the moonlight.

INSERT - THE KEYCHAIN

Engraved in a child's careful choice of font: "WORLD'S OKAYEST UNCLE. JUST KIDDING. BEST."

BACK TO SCENE

Marc laughs silently, so hard his shoulders shake. He presses the keychain to his lips.

MARC-ANTHONY
(whisper)
Best one yet.

He kisses her forehead and slips out.

CUT TO:

INT. MARC-ANTHONY'S ESTATE - STUDY - NIGHT (11:58 PM)

A modern study, glass and walnut. Awards on floating shelves. Floor-to-ceiling windows look out over the dark ridge toward the distant, glittering towers of Atlanta.

Marc enters, loosening his bow tie. He pours nothing. He sits at his desk.

His phone lights up: a flood of notifications. CONGRATULATIONS. PRESIDENT & COO. #HBDMarcAnthony. A news alert from Wall & Broad: DONOHUE NAMES BULLOCK PRESIDENT; SHARES SURGE AFTER HOURS.

And one from The Tea Kettle ATL. He reads it. He smiles slightly at the first item. He frowns slightly at the second.

"...our Tuxedo Park princess stayed home AGAIN... fourteen no-shows..."

He sets the phone face-down.

He opens the bottom drawer of the desk and takes out something new: a leather notebook, dark blue, never opened. He uncaps a fountain pen, his grandfather's gift from his Yale graduation.

He looks out the window at the city, at the lights, at the whole sleeping country beyond them.

The clock on the desk changes: 11:59 PM.

He opens the notebook to the first page.

And in one steady stroke, he writes a single word.

INSERT - THE NOTEBOOK

In bold black ink: "2028."

BACK TO SCENE

Marc-Anthony Bullock sits back in his chair and looks at what he's written. He isn't smiling. He isn't laughing.

He looks like a man who has just decided the rest of his life.

HOLD on his face in the dark, the city burning bright beyond the glass.

FADE OUT.
